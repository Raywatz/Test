# Electric Guitar: Learning Pathway (Beginner to Intermediate)

About 9–12 months at 30–45 minutes a day. Don't skip phases: each one builds the hand skills the next one needs. Move on when you can play a phase's checklist cleanly **with a metronome**, not just when the weeks run out.

---

## Phase 0: Gear and Setup (before day 1)

**What you need**
- An electric guitar. A beginner model from a known brand (Squier, Yamaha Pacifica, Epiphone) is fine.
- A small practice amp or a headphone amp, a cable, a strap, and medium picks (about 0.6–0.73 mm).
- A clip-on tuner (or tuner app) and a metronome (app or online).
- Optional but worth it: have a guitar shop do a **setup** ($40–70). It lowers the strings so the guitar is easier to play, and it's the biggest fix for "my fingers hurt and it sounds buzzy."

**Learn the basics**
- String names, from thickest (low, closest to your face) to thinnest: **E A D G B E**. Memory aid: "**E**ddie **A**te **D**ynamite, **G**ood **B**ye **E**ddie."
- Fret numbers: the open string is 0; fret 1 is nearest the headstock.
- Fingers (fretting hand): index 1, middle 2, ring 3, pinky 4.

**Reading tab** (how almost all guitar music online is written):
```
e|---------0---|   top line = thinnest string (high e)
B|-------1-----|
G|-----0-------|
D|---2---------|
A|-3-----------|
E|-------------|   bottom line = thickest string (low E)
```
The numbers are frets. Read left to right. Numbers stacked vertically are played together.

**Chord diagrams** in this plan are written low E to high e, where x = don't play the string and 0 = open string. For example, C = `x32010`.

## Phase 1: Foundations (weeks 1–4)

**Goals:** good posture, clean single notes, your first open chords, strumming in time.

**Technique**
- Press with your fingertips, just **behind** the fret wire (not on top of it). Keep the thumb behind the neck, roughly behind your middle finger.
- Hold the pick between the thumb pad and the side of the index finger, with only 2–3 mm showing.
- **Spider exercise** (do it daily to warm up): on each string, play frets 1-2-3-4 with fingers 1-2-3-4, then move to the next string. Go slowly, one finger per fret, at 60 bpm, one note per click.

**First chords** (learn two at a time):
| Chord | Shape | Chord | Shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| Em | 022000 | E | 022100 |
| Am | x02210 | A | x02220 |
| D | xx0232 | Dm | xx0231 |
| G | 320003 | C | x32010 |

**Chord-change drill:** pick two chords, set a 1-minute timer, and count how many clean switches you make. Write the number down and try to beat it every day. This is the single most effective beginner exercise.

**Strumming**
1. Down strums on each beat: 1 2 3 4.
2. Down-up eighth notes: 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &. Keep your arm moving down-up constantly, like a pendulum, even when you skip a strum.
3. The common pattern: **D – D U – U D U**.

**Songs to try:** "Horse with No Name" (2 chords), "Knockin' on Heaven's Door" (G D Am / G D C), "Wonderwall" (simplified chords), "Bad Moon Rising" (D A G).

**Checklist:** switch between any two of the 8 chords within one beat at 70 bpm; play the strumming pattern steadily for a whole song.

Your fingertips will hurt for the first 2–3 weeks, and that's normal. Short sessions several times a day are better than one long one.

## Phase 2: Rock Rhythm Basics (weeks 5–8)

**Goals:** power chords, palm muting, riffs, knowing notes on the low strings.

**Power chords** (the backbone of rock). Use only the root and the 5th, with the same shape everywhere:
```
Root on the E string (G5):      Root on the A string (C5):
D|--5--   (ring finger)         D|--5--
A|--5--   (ring finger)         A|--3--
E|--3--   (index finger)        E|--x--
```
(Many players add the octave with the pinky: E-string root at 3 → A 5, D 5.)

**Notes on the low E and A strings.** You need these to find power chords and barre chords:
- Natural notes have **no** fret between them at **E–F** and **B–C**; every other pair has one fret (a sharp/flat) in between.
- Low E string: E 0, F 1, G 3, A 5, B 7, C 8, D 10, E 12.
- A string: A 0, B 2, C 3, D 5, E 7, F 8, G 10, A 12.
- The 12th fret repeats the open string an octave higher.

**Palm muting:** rest the side of your picking hand lightly on the strings right where they meet the bridge. You should get a tight "chug." Too far forward and the notes die; too far back and there's no effect.

**Riffs to learn:** "Seven Nation Army", "Smoke on the Water", "Iron Man", "Smells Like Teen Spirit", "Come As You Are", "Brain Stew", "Back in Black" (intro).

**Checklist:** move power chords smoothly around the neck while palm-muting eighth notes at 90 bpm; name any note on the low E and A strings within 3 seconds.

## Phase 3: Lead Guitar Basics and Barre Chords (weeks 9–16)

**Goals:** the minor pentatonic scale, expressive techniques, simple solos, the blues, barre chords.

**A minor pentatonic, box 1 (5th fret).** This is the most-used scale in rock and blues:
```
e|--5--8--|
B|--5--8--|
G|--5--7--|
D|--5--7--|
A|--5--7--|
E|--5--8--|
```
Play it up and down using **alternate picking** (down-up-down-up), with index on fret 5. Then move the same shape: start at fret 3 for G minor, fret 8 for C minor, and so on (the root is the note on the low E string).

**Techniques** (learn one per week):
- **Hammer-on (h)**: pick one note, then slam another finger down on a higher fret without picking.
- **Pull-off (p)**: the reverse; flick the finger off the string to sound the lower note.
- **Slide (/ \)**: pick a note and slide to another fret while keeping pressure.
- **Bend (b)**: push the string up (or pull it down) to raise the pitch, usually a whole step (the sound of 2 frets higher). Support with 2–3 fingers. **Check that you reach the target pitch** by first playing the target note normally.
- **Vibrato (~)**: small repeated bends. This is what makes a note sound "alive." Spend real time on it.

**12-bar blues in A** (A5, D5, E5, one bar each):
`A A A A | D D A A | E D A E`
Learn the "boogie" shuffle rhythm (alternating the 5th and 6th on top of the root). Then improvise with A minor pentatonic over a backing track (search "A blues backing track"). This is where soloing begins.

**Barre chords** (they let you play any chord anywhere):
- E-shape major: 133211 (F at fret 1; move it to fret 3 for G, 5 for A).
- E-shape minor: 133111.
- A-shape major: x13331 (B♭ at fret 1; fret 3 = C).
- A-shape minor: x13321.
- Tip: roll your index finger slightly onto its bony side, and pull back with your arm instead of squeezing with your thumb.

**Solos and songs:** "Sunshine of Your Love" (riff), "Wish You Were Here" (intro), "Smoke on the Water" (solo, slowly), "Sweet Home Alabama" (intro riff), the "Johnny B. Goode" intro (as a stretch goal).

**Checklist:** box 1 with alternate picking at 100 bpm (eighth notes); in-tune bends; a 12-bar blues rhythm with a 1-chorus improvised solo; an F barre chord that rings cleanly on all strings.

## Phase 4: Understanding the Fretboard and Theory (weeks 17–26)

**Goals:** play across the whole neck and understand *why* things work.

**The major scale formula:** W W H W W W H (W = whole step = 2 frets, H = half step = 1 fret). C major: C D E F G A B C.

**Chords in a key:** build a triad on each scale note and you get the pattern **I ii iii IV V vi vii°** (uppercase = major, lowercase = minor, ° = diminished).
- G major key: G Am Bm C D Em F#°.
- The most common progressions: **I–IV–V** (G C D), **I–V–vi–IV** (G D Em C, used in hundreds of pop songs), **ii–V–I**.
- **Relative minor:** the vi chord. A minor shares its notes with C major, so A minor pentatonic also works over C major songs.

**All 5 pentatonic positions:** learn the 4 boxes that connect to box 1, one per week, then practice moving between them. Next, play the **major pentatonic**: the same shapes, just 3 frets lower (G major pentatonic = E minor pentatonic shapes).

**Triads and CAGED:** learn small 3-string major and minor triads on the G-B-e strings in three inversions. CAGED connects the open C, A, G, E and D shapes up the neck so you can see every chord everywhere.

**Rhythm upgrades:** sixteenth-note strumming, syncopation (accenting the "&"), funk-style muted strums, and playing along with recordings.

**Ear training (10 min a day):** sing intervals; figure out simple melodies (like "Happy Birthday" or TV themes) by ear on one string; use an interval-training app.

**Checklist:** name the chords in any major key; play a solo that moves through at least 3 pentatonic positions; learn one song completely by ear.

## Phase 5: Intermediate Skills and Your Own Style (months 7–12)

Choose what fits the music you like:
- **Speed and accuracy:** alternate picking with a metronome. Raise the tempo 5 bpm only after 3 clean runs. Learn economy picking and string skipping.
- **Legato:** long hammer-on/pull-off runs.
- **Modes:** Dorian (minor with a raised 6th, used in funk and Santana), Mixolydian (major with a flat 7th, used in blues-rock). Learn them as "the major scale starting on a different note," and hear each one over a drone.
- **Tone and gear:** clean vs. overdrive vs. distortion, amp EQ (bass/mid/treble), and the common pedals: overdrive, delay, reverb, wah, chorus. Less gain often sounds better and shows your technique honestly.
- **Learning full songs and solos:** pick 3 songs you love that are a bit too hard right now, and learn them over a month.
- **Recording yourself:** a phone recording is enough. You'll hear timing problems you never notice while playing.
- **Playing with others:** jam with friends or along with backing tracks in different keys.

## Daily Practice Template (40 minutes)

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 5 | Warm-up: spider exercise or chromatic runs with a metronome |
| 10 | Technique of the week (chord changes → power chords → scales/bends, depending on phase) |
| 10 | Rhythm: strumming or riffs with a metronome or drum track |
| 10 | Repertoire: the song you're currently learning |
| 5 | Fun: improvise, noodle, or play something you already know |

Habits that matter most:
1. **Use a metronome** for technique work. Playing slowly and cleanly makes you fast; playing fast and sloppy builds sloppiness.
2. **Tune up every single time** you pick up the guitar.
3. **Relax.** Tension slows you down and causes pain. Check your shoulders, thumb and picking grip.
4. **Short daily practice beats long weekly practice.**
5. Keep a log: date, tempo reached, songs learned.
